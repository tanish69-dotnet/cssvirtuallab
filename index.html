Diffie–Hellman Key Exchange

Establish a shared symmetric key over a channel an eavesdropper can read. Set the prime modulus and generator, hand each party a private number, and follow the exchange step by step until both sides hold the same secret.

  • Six guided modules, from aim to references
  • Four-step simulation with a live calculation trace
  • Primes up to 20 digits, tested with Miller–Rabin
Open experiment

Theory & formulas

The Alice/Bob protocol table and the modular proof that both parties land on the same value: Ga·b mod P.

Interactive simulation

Four steps: agree parameters, generate x and y, exchange them in public, derive the secret — with an arithmetic trace as you go.

Assessment

Five questions on the discrete logarithm problem, smooth primes and man-in-the-middle attacks. Score 4 out of 5 to unlock the references.

How the lab works

Each experiment follows the shape of a real laboratory session, so the work you do here mirrors the work you do at the bench.

  1. Step 01

    Read the aim

    Start with the objective and learning outcomes, so you know what the session is meant to prove before you touch a control.

  2. Step 02

    Study the protocol

    Theory, formulas and the step-by-step procedure — including the algebra that makes both parties agree on one key.

  3. Step 03

    Run the simulation

    Enter real parameters and advance the exchange one step at a time, watching every intermediate value as it is computed.

  4. Step 04

    Prove you understood

    A short assessment gates the references — the same way a lab submission precedes the reading list.

About the lab

“To demonstrate and verify the Diffie–Hellman Key Exchange algorithm by allowing two parties to securely establish a shared symmetric cryptographic key over an unencrypted, public communication channel.”
